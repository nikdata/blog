After two years of optimizing every ride for segment times, I realized I was dreading getting on the bike. Every ride had become a performance review. Did I beat my time on the lakefront segment? Did my average power hold up? Was my heart rate where it should be?

## The Turning Point

It hit me on a Saturday morning. Perfect weather, empty roads, and I was stressed about whether my legs felt fresh enough to attempt a PR on my favorite climb. I almost stayed home.

That was the moment I knew something had gone wrong. Cycling was supposed to be the thing that clears my head, not another source of performance anxiety.

## What Changed

I started leaving my phone in my jersey pocket. No live data, no mid-ride checks. I still record rides (old habits), but I stopped looking at segments entirely.

The result? I ride more. I ride longer. I actually enjoy it again.

## The Lesson

Not everything needs to be optimized. Sometimes the best metric is whether you smiled during the ride.
